## 1. Brand foundation

### 1.1 What the platform is

The platform enables a customer to buy a modular home made in another European country. It checks whether the selected design can legally and technically be placed in the destination market, estimates transport and installation, coordinates documentation and carries the transaction through acceptance.

Initial corridor: manufacturers in Poland; buyers and projects in Germany and the Netherlands.

### 1.2 The problem it owns

Cross-border modular construction has a certainty gap. A catalogue can show what exists, but it does not establish whether a specific building can be approved, transported, installed and accepted in a specific place.

The brand owns that gap between **available** and **admissible**.

### 1.3 Brand role

The brand is not a marketplace of inspiration and not a real-estate lifestyle brand. It is the independent clearance and transaction layer between buyer, manufacturer, authorities, logistics and assembly.

Its role is to:

1. establish feasibility before commitment;
2. make differences between markets visible;
3. assemble evidence and documentation;
4. coordinate cost, transport, installation and acceptance;
5. stop or qualify a transaction when requirements are not met.

### 1.4 Mission

Make cross-border modular housing purchases verifiable and executable before money, modules and risk move across Europe.

### 1.5 Vision

A European modular-housing market in which technical and regulatory compatibility can be established before a buyer commits and before a manufacturer enters a new country.

### 1.6 Positioning

For buyers purchasing a modular home across a border and manufacturers entering an unfamiliar market, the platform is a cross-border clearance and transaction service that verifies admissibility, documents differences and coordinates delivery through acceptance. Unlike catalogues, lead-generation portals or individual advisers, it connects the decision, evidence, cost and execution in one traceable process.

### 1.7 Brand promise

**Certainty before the border.**

This is a direction, not an unconditional guarantee. Every customer-facing claim must distinguish:

- verified fact;
- assumption requiring confirmation;
- condition to be fulfilled;
- restriction or incompatibility;
- final decision owned by an authority or third party.

### 1.8 Brand essence

**Controlled passage.**

The system lets a project proceed only when the required pieces align: project, destination rules, documents, transport, installation and acceptance.

### 1.9 Values

| Value | Behaviour | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Precision | Name the exact project, market, requirement and version | Structured dossier, dated checks, comparable costs |
| Candour | Say “not possible” or “not yet confirmed” when that is true | Visible exclusions, blockers and decision ownership |
| Composure | Reduce uncertainty without dramatizing it | Calm hierarchy, neutral language, clear next step |
| Traceability | Make every conclusion attributable | Sources, documents, status history and responsible party |
| Completion | Stay with the transaction through acceptance | Milestones from eligibility to handover |

## 2. Audiences

### 2.1 Buyer

**Profile:** purchasing a home for approximately EUR 180,000–350,000 in a country different from the manufacturer’s country.

**Primary fear:** discovering too late that the project cannot be approved, delivered at the quoted cost or accepted as expected.

**What they buy:** a defensible decision, a complete view of cost and a controlled route to acceptance.

**Message:** “Before you commit, see what is allowed, what must change, what it will cost and who is responsible.”

### 2.2 Manufacturer

**Profile:** an established modular producer entering Germany or the Netherlands without complete local regulatory, commercial and operational knowledge.

**Primary fear:** investing in sales activity and production for a project that fails on local requirements, logistics or handover.

**What they buy:** a qualified route to market and a repeatable compliance dossier.

**Message:** “Enter a new market with documented requirements, qualified projects and a transaction process you can execute.”

### 2.3 Shared emotional outcome

Both audiences buy certainty, not inspiration. The emotional shift is:

`unknown risk → visible conditions → documented decision → controlled execution`

## 3. Message architecture

### 3.1 Primary message

Know whether the project can cross the border before the risk does.

### 3.2 Supporting messages

| Message | Need answered | Required proof |
|---|---|---|
| Admissibility before commitment | “Can this exact project be built here?” | Country and project-specific assessment |
| Complete cost before movement | “What will transport and installation really add?” | Itemised, dated calculation with assumptions |
| One dossier, not scattered files | “Will every party work from the same evidence?” | Versioned document set and ownership |
| Differences made explicit | “What changes between Poland, Germany and the Netherlands?” | Side-by-side requirements and unresolved items |
| Transaction through acceptance | “Who keeps the process together?” | Milestones, responsibilities and acceptance record |

### 3.3 Short descriptions

**10 seconds**  
We verify and coordinate cross-border modular-home purchases from admissibility through acceptance.

**30 seconds**  
Buying a modular home from another country creates regulatory, transport and installation risk that a catalogue cannot resolve. The platform checks the exact project against the destination market, builds the cost and document picture, and carries the transaction through delivery and acceptance.

### 3.4 Working headline system

Use factual headlines that expose a decision or difference:

- Check the project before it crosses the border.
- One design. Different requirements.
- A complete file before a binding decision.
- Allowed here does not mean allowed there.
- Know the transport conditions before production starts.
- Green light is a status, not a slogan.

Do not lock a permanent tagline until naming is resolved. “Certainty before the border” is the current strategic line, not part of a final logo.

### 3.5 Calls to action

Preferred:

- Check project admissibility
- Start a project assessment
- Review required documents
- Compare destination requirements
- Calculate transport and installation
- View unresolved conditions

Avoid:

- Find your dream home
- Get inspired
- Start your journey
- Unlock Europe
- Buy now
- Get your green light

## 4. Voice and tone

### 4.1 Voice traits

| Trait | We are | We are not | Practical rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Precise | Specific, quantified, attributable | Pedantic or jargon-heavy | Attach a subject, market, date and condition to conclusions |
| Calm | Controlled and reassuring | Cheerful, dramatic or evasive | State the issue, consequence and next action |
| Direct | Clear about yes, no and conditional | Abrupt or simplistic | Put the decision before the explanation |
| Responsible | Honest about ownership and limits | Defensive or vague | Name who decides and what remains outside our control |
| Human | Respectful and understandable | Lifestyle-led or conversationally loose | Translate technical language without diluting meaning |

### 4.2 Voice coordinates

- Formal ↔ casual: 70% formal
- Simple ↔ technical: 55% technical, always explained
- Serious ↔ playful: 90% serious
- Reserved ↔ expressive: 80% reserved
- Assertive ↔ cautious: assertive about evidence, cautious about outcomes not controlled by the platform

### 4.3 Sentence pattern

Use this order whenever possible:

1. **Decision:** what is currently known;
2. **Reason:** the evidence or requirement;
3. **Consequence:** what it means for cost, timing or feasibility;
4. **Next action:** what must happen and who owns it.

Example:

> Conditional. The current wall assembly does not yet document the required fire classification for this German state. The project cannot move to a binding offer until the manufacturer supplies the test report. Manufacturer action required by 18 August.

### 4.4 Status language

Use one stable vocabulary across product, documents and support:

| Status | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Supported by current evidence and within stated scope | “Transport width confirmed for the proposed route.” |
| Conditional | Possible when named conditions are fulfilled | “Admissible subject to foundation redesign.” |
| Pending evidence | Decision cannot be made from available documents | “Fire report pending.” |
| Not admissible | A defined requirement is not met | “This structural system is not admissible for the selected site.” |
| Out of scope | Owned by another party or later decision | “Final permit decision remains with the local authority.” |

Never replace these with vague labels such as “looking good”, “almost there” or “should be fine”.

### 4.5 Tone by context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing | Decisive, evidence-led | “See the differences before they become changes on site.” |
| Assessment | Technical, structured | “Conditional: two documents and one design change remain.” |
| Blocker | Calm, unambiguous | “This configuration cannot proceed in the selected location.” |
| Cost update | Neutral, quantified | “Transport increased by EUR 6,400 after route validation.” |
| Success | Brief, procedural | “Assessment complete. The project can proceed to binding quotation.” |
| Support | Patient, specific | “The file is complete except for the signed structural declaration.” |

### 4.6 Vocabulary

Prefer:

- admissibility, requirement, evidence, dossier, condition;
- project-specific, destination market, verified, documented;
- transport envelope, installation scope, acceptance;
- confirmed, conditional, pending, not admissible;
- difference, responsibility, effective date, version.

Avoid:

- seamless, revolutionary, effortless, borderless;
- dream home, lifestyle, forever home, inspiration;
- guaranteed, fully compliant, risk-free, Europe-wide;
- marketplace, hub or catalogue as the category claim;
- “we take care of everything”;
- “green light” outside the literal product status.

### 4.7 Localization

English is the master language for brand governance. German and Dutch customer copy must be professionally localized, not literally translated. Regulatory terms remain those used by the relevant authority. Never translate the name of an official document into an invented equivalent; show the local name and an explanatory translation.

## 5. Naming direction

“Modular Hub Europe” is a working project label only.

### 5.1 Naming criteria

The final name should suggest at least two of these ideas:

- clearance or admissibility;
- passage between markets;
- a complete, controlled file;
- fit between a project and its destination;
- precision and accountable progress.

It should:

- work in English, German, Dutch and Polish pronunciation;
- remain credible if the company initially serves only one corridor;
- avoid claiming authority status or guaranteed approval;
- be distinctive enough for trademark and domain screening;
- support a geometric symbol without relying on initials.

Avoid names built around: hub, Europe/Euro, home dreams, eco/green, prefab catalogue, instant approval or borderlessness.

### 5.2 Naming territories for future exploration

1. **Clearance:** verified passage, permission, readiness.
2. **Fit:** project-to-market compatibility.
3. **Dossier:** completeness, evidence and traceability.
4. **Crossing:** controlled movement between systems.
5. **Module:** repeatable precision without depicting a house.

## 6. Visual idea

### 6.1 Core metaphor: the clearance gate

The identity is built from three elements:

1. a rectangular module;
2. a boundary line representing a market requirement;
3. an aligned opening or registration mark showing controlled passage.

The symbol should depict **fit across a boundary**, not speed, travel or approval theatre. It must remain meaningful without a name and legible as a one-colour 16 px mark.

### 6.2 Form language

- orthogonal geometry based on a 4-unit grid;
- rectangular modules in 1:1, 1:2 and 2:3 proportions;
- precise rules, alignment marks and cropped frames;
- square or subtly radiused corners: 0–2 units, never pill-shaped as the dominant language;
- asymmetric layouts balanced by strict alignment;
- generous negative space;
- diagrams and tables used as visual content, not decoration.

### 6.3 What the visual system must not become

- a house silhouette or pitched roof;
- a map pin, route arrow or generic globe;
- a checkmark as the primary symbol;
- a leaf, tree, sun or sustainability shorthand;
- a shield, lock or legal-services cliché;
- a collection of national flags;
- gradients, glass effects, 3D extrusion or soft “proptech” blobs.

Apply to all prices, measurements, dates, reference numbers and tables:

```css

  font-family: var(--font-data);
  font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums lining-nums slashed-zero;
  text-align: right;
```

- Keep units separate but visually attached: `6 400 EUR`, `3.20 m`, `18 Aug 2026`.
- Do not use oversized numbers as decoration unless they are also the primary information.
- Never render critical data inside images when it can remain selectable text.

## 9. Symbol and logo direction

### 9.1 Status of the design

The current symbol is a **directional concept**, not a registered final logo. The final wordmark cannot be designed until naming is complete.

### 9.2 Symbol construction

Working concept: **Clearance Gate**.

- Begin with a 4 × 4 unit square.
- Use two solid rectangular masses to define a boundary and a module-sized opening.
- Let one rectangular module align with, or pass through, that opening.
- Keep stroke and gap widths on whole grid units.
- The silhouette must survive at 16 px and in a single ink colour.
- Avoid a literal tick; clearance should be expressed by alignment and passage.

### 9.3 Required variants

1. **Horizontal:** symbol + final wordmark; primary for web headers, PDF covers and trailer sides.
2. **Symbol only:** favicon, mobile app, dossier stamp and compact UI.
3. **Monochrome:** one solid dark or reversed white version; no tints or transparency.

No stacked logo is required unless later naming tests prove the horizontal form cannot cover square use cases.

### 9.4 Clear space

Let `x` equal the width of the symbol’s internal opening. Maintain at least `1x` around the symbol and `1.5x` around the horizontal lockup.

### 9.5 Minimum size

| Context | Minimum |
|---|---:|
| Favicon | 16 px symbol, optically simplified |
| Interface symbol | 24 px |
| Horizontal digital lockup | 128 px wide |
| PDF footer | 24 mm wide |
| Dossier stamp | 14 mm symbol |
| Trailer / fair stand | Determine from viewing distance; preserve `1.5x` clear space |

### 9.6 Incorrect use

Do not:

- add a roof, chimney, leaf, checkmark, pin or flag;
- rotate the mark to imply motion;
- use status green, amber or red in the logo;
- outline, bevel, shadow or gradient the mark;
- place photographic content inside it;
- alter its grid or gap to fit a layout;
- use the working initials “MHE” as the primary concept.

### 9.7 Working-title lockups

The following lockups use “Modular Hub Europe” only to test name length and composition. They are not final wordmarks.

When the final name is selected, test it in all four arrangements before converting the approved wordmark to outlines. Do not modify the Clearance Gate geometry to compensate for a long or short name.

For large, high-impact brand moments, use `assets/brand/logo/v3/display/logo-modularhub-display-v3.png`. This display version translates the M/H monogram into an architectural, semi-isometric object with controlled depth, navy and blue material planes, soft studio light and one warm-lit glass opening.

Approved uses: website hero, trade-show wall, presentation cover, launch visual and other large display contexts.

Do not use v3 for favicon, app icon, dossier stamp, PDF footer, small interface placement, embroidery, engraving or monochrome reproduction. In those contexts use the flat v2 SVG family. The display mark is an expressive extension of the identity, not a replacement for the functional logo system.

## 10. Layout system

### 10.1 Grid

- Base unit: 4 px digital / 1 mm print.
- Spacing scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96.
- Desktop marketing grid: 12 columns, 24 px gutters, 48–80 px outer margins.
- Product grid: 12 columns, 16–24 px gutters.
- Documents: 8-column grid with a fixed metadata rail.

### 10.2 Composition

- Lead with the decision, then evidence.
- Use thin datum lines to connect labels, dimensions and objects.
- Keep at least one quiet field of negative space per composition.
- Prefer cropped industrial photography adjacent to clean data fields.
- Align labels and numbers strictly; avoid floating cards without structural purpose.
- Radius: 0–4 px for documentation and status components; 6 px maximum for interactive controls.

### 10.3 Graphic devices

Approved:

- registration marks and crop-like corners;
- module outlines and boundary rules;
- numbered dossier tabs;
- route or process diagrams built from orthogonal lines;
- document stamps using symbol + date + reference ID;
- enlarged monospace dimensions.

Not approved:

- decorative maps of Europe;
- national-flag colour combinations;
- swooshes, speed lines or travel icons;
- gradients and translucent glass cards;
- large rounded “friendly SaaS” pills as general decoration.

## 11. Photography

### 11.1 Subjects

Prioritise:

1. modules inside real production halls;
2. modules secured on trailers and passing real infrastructure;
3. cranes and installation crews during actual lifts;
4. engineers, inspectors and production staff working, not posing;
5. documents, drawings, stamps and measurements on a working table;
6. junctions, fasteners, lifting points and material details.

Exclude:

- architectural renders as documentary proof;
- families on lawns or aspirational lifestyle scenes;
- stock advisers smiling at camera;
- isolated hard-hat portraits;
- drone shots of generic suburbs;
- glossy “eco home” imagery, leaves and exaggerated greenery.

### 11.2 Treatment

- Documentary, observational and materially honest.
- Natural or industrial available light; neutral white balance.
- Moderate contrast, restrained saturation, visible metal, timber and paper texture.
- Prefer side angles and process details over symmetrical property showcases.
- People should be occupied with a real action and use correct PPE.
- Never colourise status areas of a photograph green, amber or red for effect.

### 11.3 Cropping and overlays

- Use wide functional crops or close technical details.
- Reserve negative space for factual captions or measurements.
- Overlays may use Deep Navy at high opacity; no gradients.
- Captions include location, process stage, date and whether the image is illustrative or project-specific.

## 12. Iconography and diagrams

- 24 px base grid, 1.5 px stroke at standard size.
- Square endpoints, restrained 2 px corner radius.
- Outline icons by default; filled shapes only for active or status states.
- Use literal operational symbols: document, dimension, crane, trailer, foundation, authority, signature.
- Never use a house outline as the universal project icon.
- Compliance diagrams must remain understandable in monochrome.

## 13. Applications

### 13.1 Mobile and favicon

Use the optically simplified Clearance Gate symbol. At 16 px, remove non-essential internal lines and retain one boundary, one opening and one module. Test on Warm Paper, Deep Navy and browser-default white.

### 13.2 PDF dossier

- Warm Paper cover; white interior pages when print economy requires it.
- Deep Navy metadata rail with project ID, country pair, date and version.
- Passage Blue for active section markers only.
- Footer lockup in monochrome; data and page numbers in IBM Plex Mono.
- Stamp format: symbol, assessment status text, date and dossier reference. A coloured status field is separate from the brand mark.

### 13.3 Trailer side

- Use one oversized symbol and one short factual line.
- Prefer Deep Navy field with white symbol, or Warm Paper field with Deep Navy symbol.
- Passage Blue may identify a boundary rule or reference code, not cover the entire vehicle.
- No URL hierarchy smaller than required for roadside reading.

### 13.4 Trade stand

- One proof-led headline, one real process photograph and one assessment diagram.
- Show an example difference between origin and destination requirements.
- Avoid walls of house renders or a catalogue grid.
- Provide a physical or digital dossier sample as the main demonstration object.

### 13.5 Product interface

- The decision/status is always written in text.
- Separate brand action blue from status green, amber and red spatially and semantically.
- Show evidence, owner and effective date near every compliance conclusion.
- Prices and dimensions use the data font and tabular alignment.

## 14. Accessibility and compliance

- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA as a minimum for digital interfaces.
- Never communicate status with colour alone.
- Provide alt text that describes the process or evidence, not the mood.
- Keep body copy at 16 px minimum and UI labels at 12 px minimum.
- Maintain visible focus states; focus may use Passage Blue plus a structural outline.
- Do not make legal, regulatory or “fully compliant” claims without an explicit scope, date and responsible authority.
- Photography and generated imagery may illustrate a process but must never be presented as evidence from a real customer project.

Before approval, confirm:

- the asset makes a factual, supportable claim;
- the working name is not treated as final;
- the correct logo variant and clear space are used;
- Passage Blue is the only marketing accent;
- green, amber and red appear only as labelled product statuses;
- headings, body and data use their assigned font roles;
- numerical content is tabular and aligned;
- photography shows a real process, not an aspirational lifestyle;
- the design works in monochrome and at its minimum size;
- all regulatory statements identify scope, date and decision owner;
- accessibility and required formats have been checked.
